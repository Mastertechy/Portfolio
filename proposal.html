Portfolio Website Proposal

Prepared by Gyano Dulcio

Website Purpose

This website is my professional portfolio. Its purpose is to introduce me as an Information Technology student at Broward College who is building a career in full-stack web development. It gives employers one place to learn about my background, review my technical skills, read my resume, and contact me.

I am building the site by hand with HTML and CSS and publishing it with GitHub Pages. The site itself shows that I can plan, build, and publish a complete project.

Target Audience

My primary audience is recruiters and hiring managers at companies that hire for entry-level and junior web development and IT positions. My secondary audience is internship coordinators, instructors, and professional contacts who want to learn more about my skills.

These visitors often review many candidates quickly, and many will view the site on a phone. The site needs to load fast, be simple to navigate, and put the most important information one click away.

Research: What My Portfolio Needs

I researched what employers expect from a junior developer portfolio. Based on my sources, my portfolio needs the following components:

  • An About page. Springboard lists an About Me section as a basic part of a junior developer portfolio, because employers want to know who they are considering. My About page will share my background, my career goal, and my interests.
  • Simple navigation. Springboard also advises that a junior portfolio should be easy to follow instead of a maze of pages and options. My site will have five pages and one consistent navigation menu on every page.
  • Evidence of finished work. Codecademy explains that a good junior portfolio shows employers that you can start and finish a project and apply what you have learned. My portfolio website is my first complete project, and its source code will be public in a GitHub repository.
  • A clear goal, core tools, and a way to be contacted. Codelabs Academy recommends a short bio that states your goal and main tools, a link to your portfolio site, and a contact method you actually check. My Skills page will list the tools I use, and my Contact page will include a working form.

Planned Pages

  • Home: a short introduction to who I am and what I am working toward.
  • About: my background, my goal of becoming a full-stack web developer, and my interests.
  • Skills: a table of my technical and professional skills.
  • Resume: my education, work experience, and certifications.
  • Contact: a form and contact information for employers.

This proposal page is not included in the site navigation.
